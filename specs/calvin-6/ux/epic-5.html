calvin, end to end: Epic 5 mockups

Epic 5 is "Trial-ready polish": get calvin ready for the 3–5 Bay Area trials. This sheet walks through every flow in the order a person meets it: making a plan, sharing it, committing, the confirmation and both reminders, withdrawing, both kinds of call-off, starting a new plan from an old one, and the check-in. Every state is drawn at 360px, including errors and empty ones.

The frames use the app's live CSS. The build copies today's public/app.css from testbed main into this file and only scopes it, so a frame with no changes looks exactly like the app. The few new rules are listed separately as proposed additions, and every frame that needs one says so under it. The copy is the app's own unless the frame says otherwise.

Each frame is tagged. Built means it's shown as the app draws it today. Changes means it's built but Epic 5 would change it; the changes are listed under it. New means it isn't in the app yet. After your review, stories close the gaps between this sheet and the app. A restyle story goes first, so feature stories build on the new look.

The example plan throughout is Coffee at Blue Bottle, College Ave, in Oakland (Rockridge), on Sat 14 Nov at 10:00 AM. The minimum is 3 and the decide-by time is Thu 12 Nov, 10:00 AM. Ana Ruiz makes it, and Mia Chen and Raj Patel commit.

  7. Ana calls it off

1. Ana makes a plan

Ana said "we should get coffee" in her neighbourhood chat. She opens calvin, gives a city, a rough area and a rough time, and gets one specific plan back. If the suggestion fails, she gets an empty card to fill in herself.

2. She shares it

Publishing makes Ana the first one in. She gets the link to paste into the chat, and her private manage link by email. In the chat, the link unfurls into a preview.

3. Mia and Raj commit

Mia taps the link inside her chat app. She sees the plan, who's in so far against the minimum, and a two-field form. Raj's commit fills the last seat, and the plan is on.

4. It's on, and the reminders

Everyone who's in gets the confirmation straight away, then a reminder the day before and one on the morning. Latecomers can still join until it starts. Once it starts, the page stops taking commits.

5. Raj withdraws

Every email to someone who's in, other than Ana, carries a withdraw link. Withdrawing lowers the count, but never undoes a plan that's on.

6. The deadline passes short of the minimum

Only Ana and Mia are in by Thu 12 Nov, 10:00 AM, so calvin calls it off quietly. Everyone who was in, Ana included, gets "Not this time". This is the copy the operator kept for the deadline.

7. Ana calls it off

From her private page, Ana can call the plan off until it starts, whether or not it's on. The people who were in get their own wording, which says it was Ana's call, and Ana gets no email (the operator's change to FR024).

8. Ana starts a new plan from this one

From her page, Ana starts again with the same coffee and venue on a new date. The new plan gets new links and starts with only Ana in; the old group isn't emailed.

9. Did it happen?

At 10:00 the day after a plan that was on, Ana gets two one-tap questions. Each tap records the answer and shows a thank-you; tapping again replaces the answer. This is how the trial is measured.

10. When a link goes wrong

A link cut short in a chat, or a server failure. The not-found message is the same for every kind of link, so it doesn't reveal which ones exist.

For the operator to decide

  1. The creator call-off email's subject (E6). Proposed: "Called off: Coffee on Sat 14 Nov", the same shape as "Not this time: Coffee on Sat 14 Nov". Or name Ana in the subject too: "Ana called off Coffee on Sat 14 Nov".
  2. The public page after a creator call-off (P10). Proposed: the pill stays "Not this time" and the line under it says "Ana called this one off. Nothing to do." Or keep today's "This one isn't going ahead. No harm done. Maybe next time." for both kinds of call-off, so only the email differs.
  3. "Were you there?" after "it didn't happen" (K3). As built, it's still asked. Lean: keep it, since the answer is still data; but if a gathering didn't happen, "no" is implied, so it could be skipped.
  4. "Fill in your own below" when suggestions are rate-limited (C8). The notice promises an empty card, but the page shows the inputs form. Lean: show the empty card (C7's layout) with this notice, so the promise holds.
  5. The "you?" seat after you've committed (P3). The first empty seat says "you?" even to Mia, who just committed. Lean: leave it; it's addressed to whoever opens the link next, and Mia sees her own name in the row and the notice above.
  6. Scope. Is anything here more than the trials need, or missing from them? Each "Changes" frame can be dropped on its own.

Accessibility of what's new

ContrastThe new text uses existing pairs: --muted placeholders on the field surface (6.3:1 in both themes), the lede in --ink-2 on paper (8.0:1 light, 10.5:1 dark). The disabled button is exempt, and its text still says what's happening.
Tap targetsYes/No answers are 48px tall and at least 150px wide at 360px; "Stay in" and the end-page links are 44px.
Screen readersThe waiting text stays in an aria-live region; notices keep role="status"; one <h1> per screen. On the check-in pages, the plan's name comes before the heading.
360pxNo frame scrolls sideways at 360px; long links wrap in emails and truncate in link boxes.
